# Story Stack Ladder

A generic story gets ignored. Every layer you stack on top of it moves the audience one
level deeper, from ignoring you to becoming a die-hard fan:

| # | Layer | Audience reaction | What the layer does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The moment (a bare story) | Ignore | Nothing at stake, nothing to relate to |
| 1 | **Your past** (where you started) | Curious | Gives a "before" they recognize as their own "now" |
| 2 | **Your achievement** (where you got to) | Pay attention | The gap between before and after creates tension and credibility |
| 3 | **The steps** you took | Invested | Makes the result feel reachable for them, not just for you |
| 4 | **Your learnings** | Die-hard fans | Turns your story into their lesson, which is what builds trust |

Source: Talha (@talhacoachauthority), reel DeXgkv_M7t_. Record: `reels/talhacoachauthority-2026-10-14/reel.md`.

The ladder is about **what is in** the story, not the order you tell it in. A reel can open on
the achievement and then go back to the past.

## What "present" means for each layer

Brain dumps almost always *mention* every layer in passing. What counts is whether the layer
does its job. Judge each one against these bars:

1. **The moment.** A specific scene: when, where, what happened, what was said. *Thin:*
   "I was struggling with my business." *Strong:* "Two calls in a row hung up on me before
   10am."
2. **Your past.** What life looked like before, what it cost you, and what you believed then.
   It has to match the audience's current situation, or they won't see themselves in it.
   *Thin:* "I used to be inconsistent." *Strong:* "Every Monday I'd plan the week, and by
   Wednesday I'd ghosted my own plan."
3. **Your achievement.** A concrete, observable result: a number, a before/after, or
   something you can now do that you couldn't before. It does not need to be huge, but it
   needs to be believable and specific. *Thin:* "Now things are way better." *Strong:* "12
   weeks of daily outreach without missing a weekday."
4. **The steps.** 2–4 specific actions, ideally including what didn't work and the turning
   point. Someone watching should be able to copy at least one tomorrow. *Thin:* "I just
   stayed consistent and trusted the process." *Strong:* "I cut my goal down to 5 calls a
   day and tracked them in a checkbox, nothing else."
5. **Your learnings.** The principle someone can take into their own situation, stated for
   the viewer and not just about you. It must be different from the steps: steps are *what
   you did*, a learning is *why it worked* or *what you'd tell someone starting*. Bonus if
   it's non-obvious or reverses something people believe. *Thin:* "Never give up."
   *Strong:* "Motivation didn't save the day. The next box on the list did."

## Workflow

### Mode 1: Audit (the default when the user gives a story)

1. Read the whole brain dump. Pull out every line that touches each layer.
2. Grade each layer **Strong / Thin / Missing** against the bars above. Quote the user's own
   words as evidence so they can see why.
3. Run these cross-checks, because they are where stories usually fail even when every
   layer is technically there:
   - **Audience match:** Is the "past" the audience's "now"? If the past belongs to a
     different person than the one you're trying to reach, flag it.
   - **Believable gap:** Is the jump from past to achievement explained by the steps? A big
     result with vague steps sounds like bragging. Clear steps with a tiny result sound
     pointless.
   - **Learning ≠ step:** If the "learning" is really just a step said again, flag it.
   - **One story:** If the dump holds two or three stories, name them and recommend one per
     piece. The others become a series or later posts.
4. Ask **at most 5 questions**, highest-leverage gap first, each one aimed at a single layer.
   Ask for specifics, not feelings in general: "What exactly were you doing on a typical
   Wednesday before this changed?" beats "Tell me more about your past."
5. Offer the choice: answer the questions, or build now with `[FILL IN: …]` markers where
   the gaps are.

**Never invent the user's facts.** Results, numbers, dates, names and events must come from
them. If a layer is missing, it stays a `[FILL IN: …]` marker, not something made up to sound
plausible. The technique only works if the story is real.

### Mode 2: Build (after the audit, or when the user says "just write it")

Order the layers for retention, not chronology. Default reel order:

1. **Hook:** the achievement or the before/after contrast, in one line ("12 weeks ago I
   couldn't make 3 calls in a row. This week I made 60.")
2. **Past:** the specific "before", told in the audience's language
3. **Steps:** 2–4 concrete moves, including the turning point
4. **Learnings:** the transferable lesson, said to the viewer
5. **CTA:** a light, natural one (save, comment a word, follow)

Other orders that work: open on the moment (layer 0) when it's vivid and strange, or open
with the learning as a contrarian line and then prove it with the story.

**Spreading the ladder across a series:** if the story is big, one piece per layer works too
(past → achievement reveal → the steps → the lessons). Each piece should still have a hook,
and should reference the earlier ones so people go back and watch.

## Output format

**Audit:**

```
### Story stack audit: <working title>

| Layer | Grade | Evidence (your words) | What's needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| The moment | Strong/Thin/Missing | "…" | … |
| Your past | … | … | … |
| Your achievement | … | … | … |
| The steps | … | … | … |
| Your learnings | … | … | … |

**Cross-checks:** <only the ones that fail, one line each>
**Current ceiling:** <the reaction this story earns right now: ignore / curious / attention / invested / fans>

**Fill these in (most important first):**
1. <question tied to one layer>
…
```

**Built reel (30–60 sec, about 90–160 spoken words):**

```
### <working title>
**Hook:** "<line>"
**Past:** <spoken lines>
**Steps:** <spoken lines>
**Learnings:** <spoken lines>
**CTA:** "<line>"
**On-screen text:** <short overlay>
**Caption:** <1–3 sentences>
**Layer check:** past ✓ achievement ✓ steps ✓ learnings ✓
```

For carousels: slide 1 = hook (achievement or contrast), then one slide each for past,
turning point, 2–3 steps, the learning, and CTA. For text posts: the same beats as short
paragraphs.

Write in the creator's voice: first person, conversational, meant to be spoken out loud.
Don't add hype that isn't in the brain dump.
