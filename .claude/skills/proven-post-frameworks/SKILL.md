# Proven Post Frameworks

Five repeatable post formats that reliably get engagement. Each one works for a
different psychological reason — knowing the reason is what lets you write a good
version instead of a fill-in-the-blank one.

Source: a reel by social media strategist Courtney Johnson, who has clients post one
of these whenever their account needs a boost ("it always does something,
re-triggers the algorithm").

## The 5 frameworks

### 1. "Here's how [famous person] did [thing in your industry]"
- *Examples:* How Steve Jobs organized his calendar. Taylor Swift's skincare routine.
  How Brian Chesky raised his first million.
- *Why it works:* You borrow the famous person's brand affinity — people stop for the
  name — then keep them with **your own strategic commentary**. The commentary is
  the point; a plain recap is just trivia and doesn't position the creator.
- *Pick:* someone the audience actually recognizes, doing something that maps onto
  the creator's expertise. Only use facts that are well documented; if unsure,
  flag it for the user to verify rather than inventing details.

### 2. "I've worked with [N talented people], here's what the top 1% do differently"
- *Examples:* I've worked with dozens of athletes… I've worked with 100 marketing
  teams…
- *Why it works:* It signals experience (social proof) and promises insider pattern
  recognition. Everyone wants to know what separates the best.
- *Needs:* a real number/group from the creator's own experience. If you don't know
  it, leave a `[FILL IN: how many / who]` placeholder — never fabricate credentials.
  Then give 2–4 specific, non-obvious differences with the creator's take.

### 3. "The current way of doing [X] is broken"
- *Examples:* The agile model is broken — my new model is better because… The hiring
  model is broken, and as a recruiter here's what I'm doing to fix it.
- *Why it works:* Contrarian tension creates debate, and it sets up the creator's
  product/service/method as the solution — the most direct path to leads of the five.
- *Structure:* name the broken status quo → why it fails (concrete) → the creator's
  better way → soft pointer to their offer.

### 4. "If I were to [do X] from scratch today, here's how I'd do it"
- *Examples:* If I started law school over… If I started my Instagram from scratch…
- *Why it works:* Hindsight + a promise of a shortcut. Highly saveable.
- *Structure:* a play-by-play tutorial — numbered, concrete steps, not vague advice.

### 5. Just ask a question
- *Examples:* I have one Audible credit left — what book should I listen to? What do
  you wish you'd known before starting in tech?
- *Why it works:* Audiences love giving their input; comments drive reach. Keep it
  easy to answer in one line, and ideally tied to the creator's niche so the
  answers double as research.

## Workflow

1. **Get the context.** You need the niche/topic, the audience, and the platform.
   Also useful: what the creator sells, their real experience (numbers, clients,
   background). If it's already in the conversation or project files, use it; if
   the essentials are missing, ask briefly in one message. Don't stall on optional
   details — use `[FILL IN: …]` placeholders instead.
2. **Decide scope.** Default: one idea + hook for each of the 5 frameworks, then a
   full script for whichever the user picks (or for all 5 if they asked for scripts).
   If they named a specific framework or asked for a batch/calendar, follow that.
3. **Write in the creator's voice**, first person, conversational, sounds spoken
   out loud. Short sentences. No hashtags spam, no emoji walls unless asked.

## Output format

For ideas/hooks:

```
### 1. Famous person — <working title>
**Hook (first 3 sec):** "<exact spoken line>"
**Angle:** <the creator's strategic take in one sentence>
```

For a full script (short-form video, ~30–60 sec, ~80–150 words):

```
### <Framework> — <title>
**Hook:** <line>
**Body:** <the talking points / steps, in spoken lines>
**Close / CTA:** <one line: question, follow, or offer pointer>
**On-screen text:** <short text overlay>
**Caption:** <1–3 sentences + CTA>
```

For LinkedIn or text posts, swap the script for a post: strong first line, short
paragraphs, a closing question.

End by suggesting which post to publish first and why (usually #3 if they sell
something, #5 if engagement is low and they need a quick lift).
