# Audience Timeline

A ~10-minute reflection exercise for creators in a rut. It works because the problem is
rarely "I have nothing to say" — it's "I haven't noticed how many people I already have
something in common with, or something to teach." Two lists make that visible.

## The mental model: you're in the middle of your timeline

Picture a line. You are in the middle of it.

- **Behind you** — where you've been. Lots of people are standing there right now and
  want to get to where you are. **They can learn from you.**
- **Around you** — where you are now (age, career, lifestyle, life stage, obsessions).
  Lots of people are standing right beside you. **They resonate with you.**
- **Ahead of you** — where you're going. Plenty of people share those ambitions, so
  talking about the road ahead ("I'm trying to get to X, here's what I'm figuring out")
  is relatable too.

Explain this briefly to the user before the questions — it reframes "who am I to post?"
into "there are people on every side of me."

## Run the exercise

Ask the questions one at a time and let the user answer before moving on. If they give
a short list, push for more — in the original exercise the list got *broader* as it went,
and that broadening is the moment the user realises how much they can talk about. Offer
prompting categories only if they stall; don't answer for them.

### Question 1 — Who will resonate with me the most right now?

Frame it as real life, not content: *"If you walked into a random event and met a bunch
of people, which ones would you be most excited to meet because they'd relate to you?"*
Answering from a content lens produces generic "target audience" answers; the IRL lens
produces specific, human ones.

Nudge categories if needed: career/business type, age + stage of life, past identities
(e.g. "girls who had a YouTube channel as a kid"), obsessions and interests, lifestyle
(nomadic, parent, night-shift), personality traits (highly ambitious, spontaneous).

Example list (from the source creator): marketing-agency owners, freelancers,
service-based business owners, people who had a YouTube channel when they were younger,
self-employed people in their 20s, people obsessed with content/marketing, people
obsessed with systems and automations, people who love a nomadic lifestyle, highly
ambitious women.

### Question 2 — Who can learn the most from me right now?

Prompts:
- Working professional: *what type of people come to you, and for what advice?*
- Student / younger: *what do friends come to you for — fashion, dating, career,
  academics?*
- Everyone: *what do people naturally ask you about, and what do you catch yourself
  naturally teaching?*

These are usually people standing where the user used to be.

Example list: consumer founders (on marketing), aspiring creators, existing creators
(on monetising or building an offline business), UGC creators, students interested in
an alternative path, aspiring entrepreneurs, digital nomads.

### Optional — Where am I going?

A short third pass: what the user is currently working toward. Each ambition is a
"figuring it out in public" audience.

## Turn the lists into content

Once the lists exist, deliver:

1. **The two lists, cleaned up** — grouped and deduplicated, keeping the user's specific
   wording (specificity is what makes people feel seen).
2. **Content angles per audience**, labelled by mode:
   - **Relate** (list 1 / where you're going): start a conversation about something on
     your mind — shared experiences, opinions, day-in-the-life, "anyone else…?"
   - **Teach** (list 2): speak as the authority — how-tos, mistakes you made, what you'd
     do differently, answers to the questions people already ask you.
   Give 2–3 concrete post ideas (hook-level, not full scripts) for each audience.
3. **Overlaps** — note where one post could hit several audiences at once (e.g. a
   self-employed 20-something with a nomadic lifestyle teaching systems). These tend to
   be the most distinctive pieces of the brand.
4. **A closing reminder** in one line: there is more to post about than they thought,
   purely from where they are, where they've been and where they're going.

Use this format:

```
## Who resonates with you (Relate)
- <audience> — <2–3 post ideas>

## Who can learn from you (Teach)
- <audience> — <2–3 post ideas>

## Where you're going
- <ambition> — <1–2 post ideas>

## Sweet spots (overlaps)
- <combo> — <post idea>
```

If the user wants full scripts afterwards, hand off to a scripting skill if one is
available (e.g. story or post-framework skills) rather than writing everything here.

## If the user wants you to fill it in

If they ask you to do it for them from what you already know (their bio, past posts,
earlier conversation), draft both lists, but mark it as a draft and ask them to add and
strike items — the exercise's value comes from their own recognition, and your guesses
will miss the specific past identities that make the best angles.
