# Pattern Awareness Content

**Problem awareness content is out. Pattern awareness content is in.**

Most creators remake whatever went viral: tips, hacks, "the one thing you're doing
wrong", "this is why you can't grow — you don't post enough." The viewer's reaction is
"yeah, I already knew that" — or "okay… how the hell do I actually do that?" That
content shows you understand *what performs on the platform*, not that you understand
*the person*.

Pattern awareness content instead shows the ideal client the **pattern in their own
life that creates the problem in the first place**. The problem was created by their
current level of awareness, and that same level of awareness can't solve it. So instead
of handing them another solution, you make them more aware *of themselves*.

## Why it works

- **Recognition beats information.** When a viewer sees their own behavior described
  precisely — the thing they do, the thing they tell themselves — they stop and think
  "wait a minute… that's what I do." That moment of introspection is the hook.
- **Self-awareness raises the ceiling.** When someone becomes more aware of themselves
  (not just more aware of the problem), how they see the problem and themselves
  shifts. They move out of victimhood and toward wanting to act.
- **It generates leads, not just views.** Content that proves you understand someone
  better than they understand themselves makes them want to talk to you. You don't
  need to go viral; you need the right people to feel seen.

## Problem vs. pattern — the core translation

| Problem/tip content (out) | Pattern awareness content (in) |
|---|---|
| "You can't grow because you don't post enough." | "You film three videos, hate how you sound, and tell yourself you'll redo them 'when you have more energy.' You never do." |
| "Eat protein at every meal." | "You're 'good' all day, then eat standing up at the counter at 10pm and call it a snack." |
| "Stop procrastinating." | "You don't procrastinate on everything. You procrastinate on anything where someone could say no." |

A pattern has: a **trigger** (the situation), the **behavior** (what they actually do),
the **story they tell themselves** (the justification, in their words), and the
**cost** (what it keeps them from). Specific and slightly uncomfortable is the goal.

## Workflow

1. **Know the ideal client.** Get the niche, who they are, and what they're stuck on.
   The best raw material is real: client calls, DMs, the creator's own journal or past
   self ("I used to do this"). If the creator has notes/journals available, mine them
   for patterns — the creator's own former patterns are usually the most credible.
2. **List patterns, not problems.** For each surface problem, ask: what does this person
   *do* right before the problem shows up? What do they tell themselves? When does it
   happen? Write each as trigger → behavior → self-talk → cost.
3. **Write the content** so the viewer recognizes themselves before any advice appears.
   Lead with the mirror, not the lesson. Keep advice light — one reframe or question
   is often enough; the awareness *is* the value.
4. **End with introspection or a soft door,** e.g. "If that's you, it's not a
   discipline problem — it's a pattern. And patterns can be changed." / "Comment X if
   this is you." Don't shame; the tone is "I see you (and I've been you)."

Never invent client quotes or personal anecdotes and present them as real — if you
need an example, write it as a general "you" pattern, or mark `[FILL IN: real example]`.

## Output formats

**Short-form video (~20–45 sec):**
```
### <title>
**Hook (the mirror):** "<you do X…>"
**Pattern:** <2–4 spoken lines: trigger → behavior → what they tell themselves>
**Reframe:** "<the awareness shift — what's actually going on>"
**Close:** "<introspective question or soft CTA>"
**On-screen text:** <short overlay>
**Caption:** <1–3 sentences + CTA>
```

**Carousel (6–9 slides):**
```
### <title>
Slide 1 (hook): <the pattern named in one punchy line>
Slides 2–5: <one beat of the pattern per slide — "You…", "Then you…", "And you tell yourself…">
Slide 6: <the reframe — "It's not X. It's Y.">
Slide 7: <one small awareness exercise or question>
Slide 8 (CTA): <save / share with someone who does this / comment / DM>
**Caption:** <2–4 sentences>
```

Short, punchy slide text (≤ ~20 words per slide). Carousels work especially well for
"signs you're doing X" or "the cycle" formats because each slide is one more moment of
recognition.

When giving ideas, list them as: **pattern hook → the self-talk it exposes** (one line
each), then write full scripts/carousels for the ones the user picks.
