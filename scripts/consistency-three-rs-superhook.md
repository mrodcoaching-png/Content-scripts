# The 3 R's of consistency (superhook format)

- Format modeled on: reels/DeZYXtGPeVz-2026-10-13 (Chris Chung, "How to master hooks in 42 seconds")
- Topic: behavioral consistency / taking action
- Angle: consistency isn't motivation. It's three moves: Reduce, Rehearse, Restart.
- Format: overhead notepad, diagram drawn before filming, marker taps each part as you say it. No face needed.
- Length target: ~40s (about 120 words)

## What we're borrowing from Chris's reel

1. **Superhook on line 2.** Line 1 is the normal hook. Line 2 is the reason to believe you: a result, an effort, or borrowed research.
2. **A 3-part diagram.** Triangle, three labels on the sides, the big idea in the middle, and a numbered example list underneath. Viewers stay to see every part get tapped.
3. **One concrete example per part.** He says "an example is..." every time. We do the same.
4. **"But if..." transitions.** Each part is the answer for someone the last part didn't fit.
5. **Meta close.** His on-screen title is itself a superhook ("+175k followers") and the last line points to it. Ours points to the proof in our on-screen title.

## Pick your superhook (line 2)

Use the strongest one that's true for you.

| Type | Line 2 |
|---|---|
| Results | "I've helped [FILL IN: number] clients keep a habit for 90+ days, and none of them did it with motivation." |
| Effort | "I tracked every habit I tried to build for [FILL IN: time period], and only three things decided whether it stuck." |
| Research | "Researchers at University College London found habits take about 66 days to form, and missing a single day didn't break the process." |

The research line is from Lally et al. (2009), *European Journal of Social Psychology*. 66 days was the average. The range was 18 to 254.

## Script

Everyone talks about motivation, but staying consistent actually comes down to three R's.
*(tap the middle)*

[SUPERHOOK — pick one from the table above]

The first R is **reduce**. *(tap REDUCE)* Shrink the habit until it's almost too easy to skip. An example is two pushups, not a full workout. *(tap 1)*

But if your days fall apart, you need the second R: **rehearse**. *(tap REHEARSE)* Decide before the hard moment shows up. An example is, "When I get home late, I'll do five minutes." *(tap 2)*

And the third R is **restart**. *(tap RESTART)* You will miss a day. The rule is never miss twice. One miss is a blip. Two is a new habit. *(tap 3)*

And if you want proof this works, just look at the number in my on-screen text.
