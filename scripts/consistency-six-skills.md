- Length target: ~1:45-2:00 (about 320 words, close to Sam's length). For ~60s, cut skills 4 and 6 and make it "4 skills"

## What we're borrowing from Sam's reel

1. **Pattern-interrupt opener.** He starts as if the casual viewers have already left ("now that you brain rot fiends are gone"). It filters for the serious viewer and makes them feel picked.
2. **Numbered roadmap.** "The first skill...", "which brings us to the next skill...", "the last skill...". Viewers stay to hear the rest of the list.
3. **One named, sticky idea per skill.** He has "topic, angle, format", "trail, not a rail" and "views, followers, leads". Each skill below gets its own phrase.
4. **Meta moment.** He points out the roadmap he's using in that same video. Ours points out that the word "motivation" never comes up.
5. **Comment-keyword CTA** for a template that matches the last skill.

## Full script (for practice. Don't read it word for word on camera)

Okay. Now that the people looking for another 30-day challenge have scrolled away, let me teach you the six skills that actually make you consistent.

The first skill is starting small. Most people fail because the plan only works on their best day. Shrink the habit until it's almost too easy to say no to. Two pushups. One page. Five minutes. You're not building the workout yet, you're building the person who shows up.

Which brings us to the second skill: planning for friction. You already know what knocks you off track. The late meeting, the bad night of sleep, the weekend. So decide ahead of time. "When this happens, I will do that." The decision is already made before the hard moment shows up.

Which brings us to the third skill, and it's the one nobody teaches: bouncing back. You're going to miss a day. That's not the problem. The problem is missing two. So your rule is: never miss twice. One miss is a blip. Two is the start of a new habit.

And notice I haven't said the word motivation once. That's the fourth skill: building proof instead of waiting to feel ready. Every small rep is evidence that you're someone who follows through. Motivation comes and goes. Proof stacks.

Which brings us to the fifth skill: tracking. What gets seen gets done. If you're not writing it down, you're guessing, and your brain will always remember the misses louder than the wins.

And the last skill is reviewing. Once a week, look at your tracker and ask two questions. What worked that I should keep? And what got in the way that I should fix? Keep one, fix one. That's how consistency gets easier every week instead of harder.

If you want the simple tracker I use to track all of this, comment the word TRACKER and I'll send it over to you.

## Delivery notes

- **Hook:** say it a little dry and knowing, like you're letting them in on something. Pause after "scrolled away."
- **Skill 3:** this is the riff spot. Add a real example of a time you missed once and nearly let it turn into two.
- **The meta line** ("I haven't said the word motivation once"): slow down and look straight into the lens. It's the line people will screenshot.
- **"Keep one, fix one":** count it on your fingers.
- Keep the pace up on every "which brings us to" transition so it doesn't drag.

## Before you post: the TRACKER lead magnet

You need something to send people who comment TRACKER. The simplest version is a Google Sheet with:
- one row per day, one column per habit (with the "smallest version" written in the header)
- a "missed yesterday?" flag, so never-miss-twice is visible
- a weekly review box: "Keep one: ___ / Fix one: ___"
