# Cognitive loop skits: consistency

- Format modeled on: reels/matt-bitter-2026-10-11 (Matt Bitter, "The cognitive loop can dictate your reality")
- Skill: cognitive-loop-skit
- Topic: behavioral consistency / taking action
- Length target: ~18-20s each (the original is 19s), under 60 words

## Filming (all three)

- One continuous take, speaking to the camera, against a plain background. No cuts.
- Deliver it calmly, like you're explaining your reasoning to a friend. Don't play it for laughs.
- Say the "years later" line in the same take. The "Living" label does the time jump for you.
- End right on the last word so the video loops back to line 1.

## On-screen text (all three, copied from the original's layout)

- **Title, top of screen, stays up the whole video.** There's a specific one for each skit below.
- **The stage list under the title, the same in all three videos:**

  1. Thinking
  2. Repeating
  3. Collecting evidence
  4. Identifying
  5. Deciding
  6. Living

- **Moving underline.** Underline the stage that matches the line being spoken. Line 1 is Thinking, line 2 is Repeating, and so on. Move the underline on the first word of each line.
- **No subtitles needed.** The original has none, and the list is the only text. Add them if most of your viewers watch muted, but keep them small and low so they don't fight the list.

Keeping the same list across all three makes them read as a series.

## 1. The Monday loop (strongest, so post this one first)

**Title:** How a thought keeps you starting Monday

**Script** (~18s)
1. *(Thinking)* I'll start properly on Monday.
2. *(Repeating)* There's no point starting halfway through the week.
3. *(Collecting evidence)* Had a pizza on Wednesday. Well, that's this week gone.
4. *(Identifying)* I'm just a clean-slate kind of person.
5. *(Deciding)* Better to start fresh than drag a bad week into it.
6. *(Living)* Two hundred and sixty Mondays later. Still starting.

**Prop idea:** a wall calendar behind you with every Monday circled

**Caption**
Monday isn't a fresh start. It's a way of putting it off that sounds like a plan.
The smallest version you can do today beats the perfect version you'll start next week.
Comment TODAY and I'll send you the 5-minute starter plan.

## 2. The "not a consistent person" loop

**Title:** How a thought makes you "all or nothing"

**Script** (~19s)
1. *(Thinking)* I don't know… maybe I'm not a consistent person.
2. *(Repeating)* Some people are disciplined. I'm more all-or-nothing.
3. *(Collecting evidence)* I did really well for two weeks. Then I missed a few days. Classic me.
4. *(Identifying)* I'm just not a consistent person.
5. *(Deciding)* I'll go again when I'm properly motivated. No point doing it half-hearted.
6. *(Living)* Five years later. Same goals. Same January.

**Caption**
"I'm not a consistent person" isn't who you are. It's a story you tell yourself after you miss a day.
Consistent people miss days too. They just don't miss twice.
Comment LOOP if this one hit a bit close.

## 3. The "when things calm down" loop

**Title:** How a thought keeps you waiting for the right time

**Script** (~18s)
1. *(Thinking)* Once things calm down, I'll get back into it.
2. *(Repeating)* Right now just isn't the right season.
3. *(Collecting evidence)* I tried fitting it in last month. Work blew up. Should've known.
4. *(Identifying)* I'm just someone who needs things settled first.
5. *(Deciding)* You can't pour from an empty cup. I'll rest first.
6. *(Living)* Five years later. Things never calmed down. I never started.

**Caption**
Things don't calm down. You get better at doing it when they don't.
What would your plan look like on your worst week? Start there.
Comment WORST WEEK and I'll send you how to build one.
