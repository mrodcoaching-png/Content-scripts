## Structure

The video is a list of 16 one-line rules. Each one is framed as a reversal ("X doesn't matter, Y does") and linked to the next with the same phrase every time: "which brings us to..." No B-roll section intros. The asymmetric pacing he teaches in rule 15 is used in the video itself: early rules take about 40s each, later ones take 1-2 min.

## The 16 rules

1. **Repurposing is dead.** The "waterfall" method optimizes for one platform. Pick a primary platform (he suggests Instagram and YouTube) and make content built for it.
2. **Your follower number doesn't matter.** Feeds are interest-based now, not social. Focus on serving people, views and staying relevant.
3. **The algorithm doesn't hate you, you just aren't interesting enough.** Topic is king (not gear). Check three things before you film:
   - **Topic:** has it performed before?
   - **Angle:** the same topic with a different angle performs differently.
   - **Format:** the hot formats in 2026 are "gamified" ones: rankings, tier lists, good/better/best, matters/doesn't matter, clone videos.
   It's easier to join an existing conversation than to start a new one.
4. **Your market isn't saturated, your content's too generic.** Filter every idea: is it **net new, non-obvious, nuanced, observable**? Framing counts too. Give it a unique mechanism (Dan Martell's "Buy Back" principle vs. "how to delegate").
5. **If everyone agrees with you, you're not saying anything.** Pick a hill to die on (counter-positioning / contrarian take / purple cow). Prompt: *"What are people in my niche saying that I believe at my core is wrong?"* His own example: "gear and editing matter less than topic selection."
6. **Stop making niche content.** Cover all of your ideal client's problems, not one topic. Use Paddy Galloway's **CCN fit**: every video should appeal to **Core, Casual and New** audiences. Use **progressive density**: open with a broad, relatable problem, then get denser, and end with a "wink" framework for the core audience (example: "meetings are a waste of time" → scorecards/KPIs/owners; 4M views, +10k followers).
7. **Don't monetize too early.** Post for about 6 months first. Goodwill compounds, so give, give, give, and wait until they ask you to sell. The ask should always be much smaller than what you give.
8. **Your content isn't a funnel, it's a web.** Viewers bounce between platforms. Earned audience (social) → owned audience (email, SMS, address) via lead magnets.
9. **People don't follow creators, they follow characters.** Show your humanity: family, hobbies, quirks (Dan's peanut butter).
10. **People share to signal, not to help you.** Make content people want to be *seen* sharing (e.g. "entrepreneurship is hard, stop glorifying it").
11. **Every movement needs a common enemy.** "Boss vs. leader" carousel: 10M views. The bigger the enemy, the bigger the hero. "If you can't spot the enemy, it might be you."
12. **Recognition creates loyalty.** Reply to comments, reshare tags, run giveaways and meetups, say thank you. Block 30-40 min a day for engagement.
13. **Conversation, not presentation.** Say "you", never "you guys". Talk to one person. Lower your tone; don't "MrBeast yourself".
14. **Your content isn't too long, it's too slow.** Pack the most value into the least time.
15. **Asymmetric pacing.** Make each section longer than the last (30s → 1m → 1.5m → 2m) so the video feels faster than it is (the "illusion of progress", like a progress bar that starts fast).
16. **Say old things to new people.** Repackage fundamental truths ("true regardless of external factors") in a net new, non-obvious way, then test and iterate. His short-form framework: **Hook, Explain, Illustrate, Teach** (later called Hook / Explainer / CTA / Teach).

## Takeaways for MROD content

- Rules 3-5 (topic/angle/format check, the 4-N filter, the contrarian prompt) work as a pre-script checklist for any reel idea.
- Rule 6 (progressive density) and rule 15 (asymmetric pacing) are structural choices you can apply to scripts.
- Rules 10 and 11 (signal sharing, common enemy) are hook/angle lenses. They pair well with the hook-library and pattern-awareness skills.
