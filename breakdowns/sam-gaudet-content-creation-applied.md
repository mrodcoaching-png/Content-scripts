# Sam Gaudet's 16 rules, applied to MROD Coaching

- Source video: reels/fb91XHK2YxY-2026-10-09 ("How to Become Disgustingly Good at Content Creation")
- Niche assumed: consistency and follow-through. The client knows what to do but keeps starting, stopping and restarting (the theme of scripts/consistency-question-grid.md).
- How to use this: each rule gets what he means, what it means for you, and ideas you can film.

---

## The big idea

Sam's whole video comes down to one claim: **the idea matters more than the production.** Topic, angle, format, framing and character decide whether a video works. Lighting and editing don't. So most of this breakdown is about what you say, not how you film it.

The rules fall into four groups:

| Group | Rules | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing ideas | 3, 4, 5, 16 | What should I make a video about? |
| Building the video | 6, 13, 14, 15 | How do I structure and deliver it? |
| Getting it spread | 9, 10, 11 | Why would someone share or follow? |
| The business | 1, 2, 7, 8, 12 | How does content turn into clients? |

## 1. Choosing ideas

### Rule 3: Check topic, angle and format before you film

**What he means:** Don't freestyle. Make sure the topic has worked before, pick an angle on it, and put it in a format that's working right now.

**For you:** Your topics are already proven. "Why can't I stay consistent" is one of the most searched self-improvement problems there is. You stand out through **angle** and **format**.

| Proven topic | Your angle | Gamified format |
|---|---|---|
| Staying consistent | Consistency is a recovery skill, not a discipline skill | Matters / doesn't matter |
| Motivation | Waiting for motivation is the reason you quit | Ranking: the 5 worst reasons people restart on Monday |
| Habits | Your habits aren't too small, your restarts are too big | Good / better / best |
| Falling off | Missing one day isn't the problem, what you do on day two is | Tier list: what you do after a missed day, from S-tier to F-tier |
| Discipline | You're not lazy, you're running an all-or-nothing script | Clone video: you who quits vs. you who stays consistent, talking to each other |

**Your strongest format fit:** "Matters / doesn't matter" is very close to the question grid you already make. Example:

> **Doesn't matter:** your morning routine, the perfect plan, starting on a Monday, how motivated you feel.
> **Matters:** what you do the day after you miss, whether your plan still works on your worst week, how small you're willing to start.

### Rule 4: Run every idea through four filters: net new, non-obvious, nuanced, observable

**What he means:** If the idea sounds obvious, people scroll. Give it a unique name or mechanism. (Dan Martell didn't say "delegate more." He said "buy back your time.")

**For you:** "Be consistent" and "build habits" are obvious. Your job is to **name the mechanisms** you already teach. Ideas you could make your own:

- **The Monday Trap:** treating a fresh start as a strategy instead of starting today.
- **The Floor, Not the Ceiling:** plan around your worst week, not your best.
- **The Day-Two Rule:** consistency is decided the day after you miss, not the day you miss.
- **The Restart Tax:** every big comeback costs more than the small effort you skipped.
- **All-In Syndrome:** going 100% every time you restart is why you keep restarting.

Once something has a name, every video can reference it ("this is the Monday Trap again"), and people start repeating it. That spread is the effect Sam describes in rule 5.

**Quick filter for each script:**
- Net new: would my client say "I've never thought of it like that"?
- Non-obvious: is it the opposite of the first answer they'd guess?
- Nuanced: is there a "yes, but..." in it?
- Observable: will they recognize themselves doing it this week?

The last one is your pattern-awareness edge, and it's where you're already strong.

### Rule 5: Pick a hill to die on

**What he means:** If everyone agrees with you, you're not saying anything. Find what your niche says that you believe is wrong.

**For you:** Ask yourself what the consistency and self-improvement space says that you disagree with. Possible hills (pick the ones you actually believe):

1. **"Discipline is the answer."** You might say discipline is overrated, and what people lack is a plan that survives a bad week.
2. **"Never miss twice."** You might say the real rule is "make missing cheap": the problem isn't the miss, it's what it costs you.
3. **"Go all in / 75 Hard / no excuses."** You might say extreme challenges train people to quit, because they teach all-or-nothing.
4. **"Motivation gets you started, habits keep you going."** You might say motivation is the trap, and starting without it is the skill.
5. **"Find your why."** You might say people with a strong why still quit, so the why isn't what's missing.

**Do this:** choose **one** main hill and say it over and over across 10+ videos. Sam's was "topic beats gear." Yours could be something like **"Consistency isn't discipline, it's how fast you come back."** That line can become what you're known for.

### Rule 16: Say old things to new people

**What he means:** You don't have to invent anything. Take a truth that holds no matter what and repackage it, then test, iterate and repeat.

**For you:** The fundamental truths in your niche are simple: start small, focus on recovery, not perfection, and act before you feel ready. That's fine. Each video is a **new package** for the same truth:

- Same truth (start small) in 5 packages: a question grid, a tier list, a small story (a coffee order, a missed gym day), a "you vs. you" clone video, and a pattern-awareness callout.
- Track which package performs, then make more of that package.

## 2. Building the video

### Rule 6: Progressive density (CCN fit)

**What he means:** Open with a problem everyone relates to (core, casual and new viewers), get more specific as you go, and end with a concrete framework your core audience can use.

**Applied to a 45-60s reel:**

| Section | Who it's for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0-5s) | Everyone | "You're not bad at consistency. You're bad at restarting." |
| Pattern (5-20s) | Casual + core | "You miss one day, decide the week's ruined, wait until Monday, then go all in again..." |
| Reframe (20-35s) | Core | "Consistency isn't how many days in a row. It's how fast you come back." |
| Wink framework (35-55s) | Core / buyers | "So use the Day-Two Rule: after any miss, do the smallest version within 24 hours. No catching up. No punishment. Just come back." |

The ending is what makes your future clients think "this person has a system." The opening is what gets you reach.

### Rule 13: Conversation, not presentation

- Say "you", never "you guys". Picture one specific client on the other side of the camera.
- Lower your energy. Your niche is people who feel like they've failed before, and hype makes them feel worse. A calm, matter-of-fact tone reads as "this person gets me."

### Rules 14-15: Too slow, not too long, and asymmetric pacing

- Cut anything that doesn't move the point forward. Length isn't the problem; dead air is.
- **Make each point longer than the one before.** In your question grids, row 1 can be short and punchy and the last row can carry the most explanation. The early speed makes the whole video feel fast.
- For longer videos (YouTube), keep the first section quickest: answer something in the first 30 seconds.

## 3. Getting it spread

### Rule 9: People follow characters, not creators

**What he means:** Show the human side (Dan Martell's family, sports, peanut butter).

**For you:** Your story is part of your authority. Ideas:
- Your own inconsistency history: the times you fell off, what restarted you.
- Your quirks and routines: what your "floor" looks like on a bad week.
- Recurring details people remember (a phrase, a mug, a place you film, something you always do).
- Rule of thumb: about 1 in 4 videos should tell people something about *you*, not just the method.

### Rule 10: People share to signal

**What he means:** Nobody shares to help you. They share because it says something about them.

**For you:** Ask yourself "what does sharing this say about the person who shares it?" Shareable angles in your niche:
- **"I'm someone who keeps going":** "Falling off isn't failing. Not coming back is."
- **"I've been through it":** "If you've restarted 50 times, you're not broken. You're persistent."
- **"I'm not judging, I get it":** messages people want to send to a friend ("send this to the friend who always says 'Monday'").

Put in "send this to..." CTAs when the content is relatable rather than tactical.

### Rule 11: Every movement needs a common enemy

**What he means:** The bigger the enemy, the bigger the hero. ("Boss vs. leader" got 10M views.)

**For you:** The enemy should be an **idea or system, never your client**. Options:
- **All-or-nothing thinking** (the strongest one: it's the villain behind the Monday Trap and All-In Syndrome).
- **"Start Monday" culture.**
- **Hustle/no-excuses influencers** who make normal people feel lazy.
- **30-day challenges** built to be quit.

**Format idea, "Boss vs. leader" style:** "All-or-nothing you vs. consistent you"
- All-or-nothing you misses a day and quits the week. Consistent you does 5 minutes.
- All-or-nothing you waits for Monday. Consistent you starts with something small today.
- All-or-nothing you sets a perfect plan. Consistent you sets a floor.

That's a carousel, a reel and a clone video from one idea.

## 4. The business

### Rule 1: Platform-first content, not repurposing
Pick a primary platform (probably Instagram for you, based on the reel-focused work in this repo) and build for it first. Reuse the **idea** on other platforms, but rebuild the video.

### Rule 2: Follower count doesn't matter
Judge each video by views, saves, shares and DMs, not by follower growth.

### Rule 7: Don't monetize too early (give, give, give)
Build goodwill before you ask. That doesn't mean never mentioning coaching. It means most videos should give a complete, usable idea with no strings attached. When you do ask, keep it small (a free resource or a DM keyword) before pitching coaching.

### Rule 8: Content is a web, not a funnel
Give people a way to move from social to something you own:
- **Lead magnet idea:** "The Floor Plan": a one-page worksheet to set the minimum version of each habit for your worst week.
- **DM keyword CTA:** "Comment FLOOR and I'll send it to you." This fits your current reel format.
- Then follow up by email.

### Rule 12: Recognition creates loyalty
Block time every day to reply to comments and DMs. Reshare stories where people tag you with their "floor" or their day-two comeback. This turns into user-generated proof that the method works.

## Pre-script checklist (use on every idea)

- [ ] Is the topic proven (people already talk about it)?
- [ ] Is my angle different from the usual advice?
- [ ] Is it in a format that's working (ranking, tier list, matters/doesn't, clone, grid)?
- [ ] Net new, non-obvious, nuanced, observable?
- [ ] Does it support my one hill to die on?
- [ ] Is there a common enemy (an idea, not a person)?
- [ ] Would someone share it to say something about themselves?
- [ ] Hook for everyone, ending for my core audience?
- [ ] Am I talking to one person, at a calm tone?
- [ ] Is each section a little longer than the one before?
